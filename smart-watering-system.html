Smart Watering System

A system that measures a plant's conditions, waters it automatically, and sends its data to a website every ten minutes.

TeamGroup N5: Riley Beaudion, Max Thorn, Will Grundig
CourseENGR 111 L, Stevens, December 2025
My rolePrimary on circuit building, application and testing; secondary on coding
Print time6 hours 51 minutes (limit 8 hours)
Size limit5 x 5 x 10 inches

The challenge

Design a system that takes measurements from a potted plant and uses them to decide when to water it. The design had to print in under 8 hours on an Adventurer 3 Series printer, fit within 5 x 5 x 10 inches, secure every component, and work as one unit.

Concept design

The first design had a front slot for the water bottle and a hollowed back for electronics, but it did not fit the size limits. The second added a front extrusion for the bottle, slots for the components and a pump slot on the side, but it left too little room. The final design came from rethinking both.

Mechanical design

In the final design, each component has its own place. A front bar holds the water bottle, the pump sits in a side slot, and a shelf on the back has slots for the breadboard, ESP32 and motor controller.

Electrical design

We followed a color code with every ground wire black. GPIO pins connect to the motor controller so the pump can also be run manually. The light sensor and an ADC pin share a row on the breadboard to get readable light values, and the humidity sensor shares a row too.

Debugging the sensors

The circuit was correct on paper and the code checked out, but the DHT11 and soil moisture sensors would not work when plugged in together. Each worked alone. After about an hour of trying wiring combinations, we found they had to share the same power supply and the same ground line.

A second problem was getting the pump to respond to the soil sensor at 30% moisture. Our group was new to coding, so we asked for help, reviewed earlier assignments and worked it out.

Software

The code publishes four MQTT topics for soil humidity, air humidity, temperature and light. It uploads every ten minutes and waters with 20 mL every 24 hours, and also whenever soil moisture reads under 30%.

Prototyping

The first prototype held the water container on pedestals, which was not secure, and its components were loose with cluttered wiring. For the second version we lowered the container into a slot at the base, added slots to hold the boards, and cut openings in the wall for wire routing, which also cut print time.

Printing took three tries: the first ran out of filament, the second failed because the supports were not strong enough, and the third came out as planned.

Data collected

We collected data from December 5 to 9, 2025, across five days of deployment, three in a row and two not. Light read zero over the weekend, which we think was because the lab lights were off. Temperature and humidity dropped to zero when we disconnected the system for our presentation.

Reflection

Building a working system in our first semester with little experience taught us the engineering design process, teamwork and problem solving. If we did it again, we would spend more time on the original model design so it needed fewer changes later.
